# Counterpunch

A discipline of the interval — work in which the enclosed space is cut before the form around it, and weight is judged by what remains rather than by what is laid down.

## The philosophy

Counterpunch holds that a mark is described entirely by the space it fails to occupy. The eye does not measure a stroke; it measures the gap beside the stroke, and revises its judgement of thickness from that gap alone. Work made under this discipline therefore begins on the inside. The void is struck first, deliberately and at full size, and the material is brought inward to meet it until the interval is exactly right. This is slow. It cannot be hurried, and it cannot be approximated, and the difference between an interval that is correct and one that is nearly correct is visible instantly to anyone who has spent years looking. Every piece must carry the evidence of those years.

## Form, space, weight

Form appears as a graded series rather than a single statement. One shape is repeated across an axis, changing by increments so small that adjacent members look identical and distant members look unrelated — the whole range legible only when the series is seen entire. Space is structural, never leftover: the field around the figures is measured with the same care as the figures, and the margin is understood as the largest counter on the sheet.

Weight is the subject and must never be decorative. It is distributed along the axis with absolute evenness, so that the progression reads as measurement rather than as gesture. Where the series closes — where the counters finally shut and the form becomes a solid — that limit is not hidden. It is the most honest moment in the work, and a master leaves it visible.

## Colour and material

Colour is a closed system: one ground of proofing stock that holds light without glare, one deep ink that sits in the surface rather than on it, and a single flame used exclusively as a point of registration — never as a field, never as emphasis, never more than a few square millimetres on the entire sheet. The restraint is the statement. Anyone can add a second colour; refusing to is the harder and more expert act.

## Rhythm and composition

The rhythm is metric. Elements sit on a rule, tick against it, and repeat at trusted intervals, in the manner of a specimen sheet or a calibration chart. Density accumulates through patient repetition of one element at many values, not through variety. Small clinical annotations occupy the margins — numerals, deltas, registration ticks — set so quietly that they read as instrumentation rather than as text, and reward only the viewer who leans in.

Typography serves as instrument. A single restrained display line may anchor the sheet; everything else is whispered in monospace at the edges, aligned to the same grid as the figures, never crowding them and never explaining what the figures already show. Nothing overlaps. Nothing touches an edge it was not measured against.

## The standard

A piece of Counterpunch must look as though it was cut by hand, checked under a loupe, and corrected over many evenings by someone at the absolute top of the craft. It should read first as a clean and quiet object and only later reveal the density of decisions inside it. If it can be improved by adding, it is not yet refined; if it can be improved by removing, it is not yet finished. The labour must be evident in the precision and invisible in the effort.
